ADS305 Big Data Analytics, Week 10
Lab 8 (Regression), Lab 9 (Classification) and Lab 11 (Cross-Validation)

HOW TO USE THESE FILES

Lab 8 and Lab 11 each arrive as a zip. Unzip each one into a folder of its own, and
keep everything inside that folder where it is.

The notebooks in those two labs read their data and their images from folders beside
them, so they only run if those folders come with them and stay where they are. If you
open a notebook from inside the zip without unzipping it, or take the notebook out on
its own, the images show as broken and the notebook stops at the first cell that loads
data, and nothing is wrong with your Python.

Lab 9 is different. It is one notebook with nothing beside it, so it is not zipped and
it can live anywhere on your machine.

THE ONE THING THAT BREAKS IT

In Lab 8, keep lab08.ipynb beside data and images. The notebook refers to ./data/ and
./images/, which means "the folder called data, or images, next to me".

In Lab 11, keep notebook, data and fig side by side. The notebooks refer to ../data/
and ../fig/, which means "go up one level from the notebook folder, then into data, or
into fig".

Move a notebook somewhere else, or flatten the folders into one, and those paths stop
resolving.

HOW THE SLIDES POINT AT A CELL

The slides name cells by number, and so does this file. The number counts every cell
from the top of the notebook, text cells included, starting at 0. It is not the number
Jupyter prints in square brackets beside a cell, which only records the order things
were run in. Most slides also show a line or two of the cell's own code with its number
beside it, so the quickest way to find a cell is to look for that line.

Every slide has a notes page, written to you. The slide is the short version and the
notes carry the rest, so open the notes pane and read both.
